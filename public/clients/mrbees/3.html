Our Fields

Seventeen acres
of living brewery

"Every ingredient on this estate has a root system. We don't import flavour — we cultivate it."

Founded in 1994 in a converted grain barn on the edge of Faversham, Mr. Bee's has never treated craft brewing as a performance. The chalk aquifer beneath the meadow gives our water a mineral softness no filtration plant can replicate. Our six Langstroth hives supply raw wildflower honey in a narrow August harvest window that lasts, at most, eleven days.

The result is beer with a genuine postcode — flavours that shift subtly year to year as the meadow does, as the seasons do. That's not inconsistency. That's honesty.

What goes in

Five ingredients,
all with roots

We don't buy flavour concentrate or shortcut the natural processes that give each brew its character. Every ingredient is traceable to a specific field, spring, or apiary — most within twelve miles of the brewery.

🍯
Estate Apiary · August Harvest
Raw Wildflower Honey
From six Langstroth hives at the wood's edge, where clover, borage, and bramble flower in succession. Harvested across an eleven-day window when the nectar is at its most complex. Folded into the boil at flame-out, never before — preserving the volatile aromatics that make each batch unique.
🌿
Faversham · Neighbouring Estate
East Kent Goldings
Grown under the same family for four generations. Resinous, softly floral, irreducibly English. Whole-cone only — never pelletised.
💧
On-Estate · Chalk Aquifer
Spring Water
Drawn from a chalk aquifer beneath the meadow. Mineral-soft and naturally filtered over centuries — the invisible architecture of every brew.
🌾
Crisp Malting · Great Ryburgh
Maris Otter Barley
The oldest barley variety in continuous commercial production. Biscuit warmth, slight nuttiness, and a clean backbone that lets the honey speak. Malted at Crisp's Victorian floor maltings.
✦
Maintained Since · 1994
Heritage Yeast
A house strain kept in continuous propagation since the brewery's founding. It carries the estate's microbial fingerprint — the closest thing we have to terroir.
